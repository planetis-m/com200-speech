# Speaker Notes

## Slide 1 — Title

**Core message:**  
This is not about bad politicians.  
It is about **power**.

**Delivery rhythm:**  
Start slow.  
Let the title settle.

Look at the audience first.  
Then begin.

**Talking path:**  
Democracy can remain visible  
while citizen control disappears.

The institutions stay.  
The ritual stays.  
The vocabulary stays.

But the **power** moves elsewhere.

**Visual anchors:**  
Title: The Republic Is Not Enough  
Subtitle: Oligarchy Behind Representation  
Visual: parliament image

Democracy can disappear while still looking **legal**.

## Slide 2 — Scale of Democratic Crisis

**Core message:**  
The crisis is not collapse.  
It is **erosion**.

**Delivery rhythm:**  
Measured tone.  
Point to the indicators one by one.

Pause after the score.

**Talking path:**  
Greece still holds elections.  
Greece is still classified as free.

But freedom on paper  
does not mean healthy democracy in practice.

The warning signs are visible.  
Trust pressure.  
Corruption concerns.  
Media pressure.  
Citizen distance.

This is how democratic decline often works.  
Not all at once.  
Layer by layer.

**Visual anchors:**  
Left column: crisis keywords  
Stat card: 85/100  
Footer: Freedom House

**Emphasis moment:**  
Erosion is dangerous because it feels **normal**.

## Slide 3 — Argument 1: Elections ≠ Democracy

**Core message:**  
Voting is necessary.  
But voting is not **sovereignty**.

**Delivery rhythm:**  
Calm.  
Analytical.

Use short questions.  
Pause after each one.

**Talking path:**  
A ballot gives citizens a voice.  
But does it give them control?

Can citizens set the agenda?  
Can they initiate laws?  
Can they recall officials?  
Can they stop harmful legislation?

If not, voting becomes permission.  
Not rule.

A democratic system must keep citizens present  
after election day.

**Visual anchors:**  
Title: Elections ≠ Democracy  
Image: citizen assembly  
Names: Aristotle · Rousseau · Dahl

**Emphasis moment:**  
A vote without control is only **permission**.

**Transition:**  
When control disappears, representation starts to reverse direction.

---

## Slide 4 — Argument 2: Representation Gap

**Core message:**  
Representation has become a **filter**.

**Delivery rhythm:**  
Slightly faster.  
Build momentum.

Point down the funnel.

**Talking path:**  
Before citizens vote,  
the field has already been narrowed.

Parties choose candidates.  
Party leadership shapes the message.  
Party discipline controls behavior.

Citizens enter late.  
Citizens choose from pre-approved options.

Then policy moves downward again.  
From government.  
To parliament.  
To citizens.

That is managed consent.

**Visual anchors:**  
Funnel: Citizens → Parties → Parliament → Policy  
Keywords: gatekeeping, discipline, weak mandate

**Emphasis moment:**  
Citizens are asked to approve choices already **filtered**.

**Transition:**  
Once representation weakens, organized interests fill the space.

## Slide 5 — Argument 3: Elite Capture

**Core message:**  
Elite capture is structural, not **secret**.

**Delivery rhythm:**  
Restrained.  
No outrage.

Let the number carry weight.

**Talking path:**  
This is not about hidden control over every decision.

It is simpler than that.  
Power follows access.

Access follows money.  
Media reach.  
Party position.  
Administrative control.

When those concentrate,  
citizens remain formally sovereign  
but practically distant.

Corruption risk is not just a moral problem.  
It is a democratic design problem.

**Visual anchors:**

**Emphasis moment:**  
Power follows **access**.

**Transition:**  
The media data makes that distance even clearer.

## Slide 7 — Satisfaction: Democratic Party

**Core message:**  
The solution is a party designed to return **control**.

**Delivery rhythm:**  
Confident.  
Practical.

No utopian tone.  
Use a circular hand gesture.

**Talking path:**  
A democratic party cannot just use democratic language.  
It has to operate democratically.

Open primaries.  
Citizen referenda.  
Legislative initiatives.  
Recall.  
Transparent financing.

These are not slogans.  
They are control mechanisms.

The order of politics must reverse.

First, citizens shape policy.  
Then representatives carry the mandate.

**Visual anchors:**  
Circular model: Citizens ↔ Policy ↔ Representatives  
Bullets: primaries, referenda, recall  
Keyword: transparency

**Emphasis moment:**  
Structure matters more than **branding**.

**Transition:**  
Now picture the difference between doing nothing and building this.

## Slide 9 — Action: Audience Role

**Core message:**  
Democracy starts after the **vote**.

**Delivery rhythm:**  
Direct.  
Eye contact.

Use each bullet as one step.  
Not as a list to read.

**Talking path:**  
Join civic forums.  
Not someday. Now.

Demand internal democracy from parties.  
Ask how candidates are chosen.

Support recall and referenda.  
Track transparency reports.

Most importantly,  
ask every political movement one question:

How will citizens control you  
after you win?

That question changes the standard.

**Visual anchors:**  
Checklist visual  
Bullets: join, demand, support, track, organize  
Bottom line: voting is the beginning

**Emphasis moment:**  
Voting is the **beginning**.

**Transition:**  
The sources support the argument, but the responsibility belongs to us.
